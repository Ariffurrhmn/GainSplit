# HIGH LEVEL SUMMARY
---
## Summary
This paper presents a rigorous, pre-specified empirical comparison of ten semi-supervised routes for low-label sarcasm detection on the News Headlines dataset across label budgets ranging from 1% to 100%. The evaluated methods span generative adversarial networks (GAN-BERT), latent diffusion models (used as generators and as consistency regularizers), LLM-generated synthetic headline pairs, sentiment and emotion multi-task helper heads, Fast Gradient Method (FGM) adversarial training, self-training, and continued self-supervised pretraining via Masked Language Modeling (MLM) and a text-adapted Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA). The authors demonstrate that simple continued MLM pretraining on the unlabeled task headlines outperforms every other approach (+7.4 macro-F1 points at 1% labels and +2.8 points at 10%), absorbs the standalone gains of helper tasks, FGM, and self-training, and matches plain BERT trained on ten times more labeled data. Crucially, through diagnostic experiments using outlet-word masking and zero-shot cross-dataset transfer to `iSarcasmEval`, the paper bounds its own positive findings by proving that the gains reflect accelerated acquisition of dataset-specific publisher shortcuts (*The Onion* vs. *HuffPost*) and in-distribution label efficiency rather than generalizable sarcasm comprehension. The manuscript is fully compliant with double-blind review requirements and connects directly to prior work presented at IEEE QPAIN.

## Strengths
* **Rigorous Controlled Ablation of Complex Semi-Supervised Architectures**: The paper provides a highly valuable methodological corrective by isolating ten semi-supervised and generative routes under a unified encoder, deterministic multi-seed protocol, and paired bootstrap significance testing with Holm's step-down correction. It convincingly demonstrates that inexpensive task-adaptive MLM pretraining dominates complex generative and multi-loss pipelines while absorbing the standalone gains of FGM, self-training, and auxiliary heads.
* **Insightful Mechanistic Deconstruction of Affective Helper Tasks**: Even without the exploratory polarity-flip placeholder in Section IV-D, the diagnostic argument explaining why sentiment and emotion helper heads barely help is complete and compelling. Showing that randomly shuffled helper labels retain 72% of the multi-task gain (+1.17 of +1.63 macro-F1 points at 1% labels), that teacher affect probabilities carry nearly zero direct predictive signal for headline sarcasm (AUC 0.546), and that MLM pretraining renders their gain statistically insignificant effectively refutes the assumption that zero-shot auxiliary affect heads succeed by injecting semantic affective knowledge.
* **Exemplary Claim Calibration and Diagnostic Bounding**: The authors commendably bound their own headline gains using controlled outlet-cue masking and cross-dataset transfer to author-labeled sarcasm. By showing that continued MLM pretraining relies on publisher vocabulary shortcuts at the exact same rate as standard BERT and fails to transfer zero-shot, the paper prevents readers from conflating high benchmark scores on source-confounded data with genuine pragmatic understanding.

## Weaknesses
* **Asymmetry in Unlabeled Data Exposure and Compute Budgets Across Routes**: Because fine-tuning is locked to $\max(200, 10 \text{ passes over } L)$ steps at batch size 32, single-stage joint semi-supervised baselines like GAN-BERT and the combined multi-task model see less than 28.4% of a single pass over the unlabeled pool $U$ at the 1% budget (and $<85\%$ even in the $3\times$ step check), whereas MLM and JEPA receive 10,000 dedicated pretraining steps (~28 full passes over $U$) prior to fine-tuning. The authors might consider either evaluating single-stage routes across multiple full passes over $U$ (matching the original GAN-BERT epoch structure) or explicitly framing the comparison as showing that an inexpensive two-stage pretraining pass beats standard fixed-step joint fine-tuning rather than claiming an exposure-matched comparison (Reference Paper: GAN-BERT: Generative adversarial learning for robust text classification with a bunch of labeled examples, Danilo Croce, Giuseppe Castellucci, Roberto Basili, 2020).
* **Restriction of Helper-Head Supervision to the Scarce Labeled Set $L$**: In Arm A2 ("Helper heads on $L$"), auxiliary sentiment and emotion heads are trained solely on the tiny labeled subset $L$ (228 headlines at 1%), even though teacher pseudo-labels are available at zero human annotation cost across the entire 22,572-example unlabeled pool $U$. While Section IV-D convincingly shows that the teacher labels carry minimal task signal, please consider clarifying whether the combined GAN-BERT + helper heads re-implementation (Arm A5) applies helper losses to $U$ as well as $L$, and briefly explaining why A2 was restricted to $L$ rather than evaluated across $L \cup U$ (Reference Paper: Enhancing sarcasm detection using GAN-BERT with multi-task learning, Afif Hossain Irfan, S. Das, Jannatul Ferdaus, M. T. Ahammed, 2025).
* **Tension Between the General Sarcasm Framing in the Title and the Single Outlet-Confounded Testbed**: Because the 10-route low-label comparison is conducted solely on *News Headlines*—where labels equal the publishing outlet and token-level MLM is uniquely suited to learning lexical style shortcuts—the unqualified phrase *"for Low-Label Sarcasm Detection"* in the title reads slightly more broadly than the paper's well-bounded conclusions. Please consider either narrowing the title slightly to reflect headline/benchmark specificity (e.g., *"...for Low-Label Headline Sarcasm Detection"*) or explicitly emphasizing in the Introduction and Limitations that the testbed was selected specifically to audit recent semi-supervised claims on this benchmark rather than an unconfounded in-domain corpus (Reference Paper: Generalizable sarcasm detection is just around the corner, of course!, Hyewon Jang, Diego Frassinelli, 2024).
* **Positioning of the TAPT + Self-Training Absorption Finding and Consistency Baseline Scope**: The factorial result showing that MLM pretraining absorbs self-training (+0.35 points, not significant) directly contrasts with prior NLP findings that Task-Adaptive Pretraining and self-training are strongly complementary, which would be valuable to discuss alongside why teacher accuracy saturates on this two-outlet pool; similarly, please consider clarifying that the negative consistency result applies specifically to continuous `[CLS]`-diffusion perturbations rather than discrete text-level Unsupervised Data Augmentation (Reference Paper: Task-adaptive Pre-training and Self-training are Complementary for Natural Language Understanding, Shiyang Li, Semih Yavuz, Kazuma Hashimoto, Jia Li, Tong Niu, Nazneen Rajani, Xifeng Yan, Yingbo Zhou, Caiming Xiong, 2021; Reference Paper: Unsupervised data augmentation for consistency training, Qizhe Xie, Zihang Dai, Eduard Hovy, Minh-Thang Luong, Quoc V. Le, 2020).
* **Statistical Protocol Details, Incomplete Multi-Budget Reporting, and Pretraining Seed Parity**: The manuscript defines Holm's correction over pre-planned contrast families without stating the exact family sizes $m$, reports tabular results only for 1% and 10% despite specifying five budgets (1%, 5%, 10%, 30%, 100%), and compares three MLM pretraining seeds against a single JEPA seed (where JEPA also faces a cold-start disadvantage from training an uninitialized predictor head from scratch vs. BERT's native warm-started MLM head). Please consider specifying the contrast family sizes $m$, reporting or summarizing the 5%, 30%, and 100% scores for key baselines, and noting the warm-start and seed asymmetry between MLM and JEPA.

## Potential Issues And Suggestions
### [Framing, Claim Scope, Related Work, Venue Fit & Anonymity/Bibliography Audit] (Pages: 1-2, 4-5)
#### Potential Mistakes and Improvements

1. **Claim Scope and Title Calibration vs. Downstream Diagnostic Evidence**
   * **End-to-End Verification of Bounded Claims:** The explicit scope boundaries stated in the Abstract, Section I (Contribution iii), and Section VI (Conclusion)—that the gain from continued Masked Language Modeling (MLM) pretraining reflects dataset-specific label efficiency rather than generalizable sarcasm comprehension—are directly and quantitatively substantiated by the diagnostic checks in Section IV-E. Specifically: (a) under the 200-word outlet-cue masking test (*The Onion* vs. *HuffPost*) compared against random word masking, every BERT variant loses roughly 3 macro-F1 points more under cue masking, and the MLM-pretrained encoder exhibits virtually the exact same reliance gap as plain BERT ($\Delta = -0.07$ points at 1% labels and $-0.17$ at 10%, neither statistically significant); and (b) in zero-shot cross-dataset evaluation on author-labeled tweets in *iSarcasmEval*, all pretrained arms remain near random chance (ROC-AUC $47.8$–$57.3$), falling well below both in-domain BERT ($65.8$) and a trivial word-count baseline ($68.7$). In addition, every headline numerical figure in the Abstract, Introduction, and Conclusion ($+7.4$ points at 1%, $+2.8$ points at 10%, $\approx 1.6$ points each for helper heads/FGM/self-training at 1%, $+1.0$ point combined on top of MLM at 1%, and statistical equivalence between 1% MLM and 10% plain BERT) matches Table I, Table II, and Section IV exactly.
   * **Potential Concern Regarding Title Scope:** The current title (*"Just Keep Pretraining: Unlabeled Headlines Beat GANs, Helper Tasks and LLM Data for Low-Label Sarcasm Detection"*) is accurate insofar as the benchmark task is standardly termed "sarcasm detection" in the motivating literature [1, 3] and the phrase *"Unlabeled Headlines"* signals the text domain. However, because the paper's own Q3 diagnostics demonstrate that models trained on *News Headlines* primarily learn publisher-specific lexical shortcuts faster and fail to transfer to author-labeled sarcasm, a critical reviewer reading the title in isolation might view the unqualified phrase *"for Low-Label Sarcasm Detection"* as slightly broader than the paper's concluding warning (*"High scores on this dataset should not be read as sarcasm understanding"*). While the final sentence of the Abstract immediately bounds this claim, if the authors wish to make the title completely self-contained against scope critiques while preserving its comparative punch, a minimal adjustment such as *"...for Low-Label Headline Sarcasm Detection"* or *"...on the News Headlines Sarcasm Benchmark"* would explicitly bind the title to the benchmark.

2. **Related Work Positioning and Early Baseline Contextualization (Sections I and II)**
   * **Contextualizing the Seed Paper's Label Budget Early (Section I):** Section I motivates the study by noting that semi-supervised learning promises to compensate for scarce labels, immediately citing the recent combined GAN-BERT and multi-task model [3] that reports $93.88\%$ accuracy on *News Headlines*. However, it is not mentioned until the final sentence of Section V (Limitations) that [3] evaluated its model using $70\%$ of the labels (and reported accuracy rather than macro-F1). A reviewer reading Section I alongside Table I (where the re-implemented combined model A5 scores $78.1\%$ macro-F1 at 1% labels and $88.4\%$ at 10% labels) may initially be puzzled by the numerical gap between $78.1\%$–$88.4\%$ and $93.88\%$. Mentioning in the opening paragraph of Section I that [3] reported $93.88\%$ using a $70\%$ labeled split—whereas semi-supervised methods are primarily needed when labels are genuinely scarce ($1\%$–$10\%$)—would sharpen the motivation and prevent baseline confusion early on.
   * **Precedent on Combining TAPT and Self-Training (Section II, IV-C, and V):** A central finding highlighted in the Abstract, Section I (Contribution ii: *"absorbs the gains of the other routes"*), Section IV-C (Table II), and Section VI is that continued MLM task-adaptive pretraining (TAPT) absorbs the standalone gain of self-training (`S` adds $+1.62$ points on plain BERT at 1% labels, but only $+0.35$ macro-F1 points on top of MLM, not statistically significant, representing a robust negative interaction shrinking the gain by $1.27$ points). In the broader NLP semi-supervised literature, a well-known precedent (Li et al., *"Task-adaptive Pre-training and Self-training are Complementary for Natural Language Understanding,"* Findings of EMNLP 2021) argued that task-adaptive pretraining followed by fine-tuning and self-training (the TFS protocol) yields strong additive gains across NLU tasks. Briefly noting this precedent in Section II (*Semi-supervised text classification*) alongside [4] and [5] and contrasting it with the diagnostic in Section IV-C/V would strengthen Contribution (ii) by highlighting why self-training saturates here: because both MLM pretraining and self-training operate on the exact same closed 22,800-headline pool $U$ on a binary two-outlet dataset, TAPT alone pushes teacher pseudo-label accuracy to $92\%$–$96\%$ on the hidden training labels in $U$, leaving minimal unlearned signal without an external unlabeled corpus.
   * **Distinguishing Latent Diffusion Consistency from Text-Augmentation Consistency (Section II):** Section II introduces consistency training by citing Unsupervised Data Augmentation (UDA) [8] (*"consistency training asks for the same prediction on perturbed inputs [8]"*). Whereas UDA [8] applies discrete text-level augmentations (such as back-translation), the consistency route evaluated in this paper (Arm A25) perturbs pooled `[CLS]` vectors via a latent diffusion model. In the Abstract, Introduction, and Conclusion, the negative result is properly scoped to *"diffusion consistency,"* avoiding any overclaim. Still, briefly clarifying in Section II or Section VI that the tested consistency route tests continuous latent-space diffusion perturbations rather than discrete token/text augmentations [8] would preempt NLP reviewers from asking why text-level UDA was not the consistency baseline.

3. **Venue Fit and Framing for a Generalist AI/ML Audience (IEEE QPAIN 2027)**
   * **Suitability and Direct Continuity:** Submitting this study to the AI and ML track of IEEE QPAIN 2027 is well-motivated because the primary composite baseline under evaluation—the combined GAN-BERT with sentiment and emotion helper tasks [3]—was published at IEEE QPAIN 2025. Calling out in Section I that [3] was introduced at QPAIN 2025 makes the direct continuity and relevance to the venue's readership immediately apparent to track chairs and reviewers.
   * **Anticipating Non-NLP Reviewer Questions:** Because IEEE QPAIN is a broad, photonics-led interdisciplinary conference where reviewers in the AI and ML track may be general machine learning researchers rather than computational linguistics specialists, addressing three minor framing points in Sections I, II, V, and VI will help preempt common generalist questions:
     1. *Why evaluate on News Headlines at all if its labels equal the publishing outlet?* When a generalist reviewer reads in Section II (*Shortcuts and transfer*) that *"In News Headlines the label equals the outlet (The Onion vs. HuffPost)"* and in Section VI that future work *"should evaluate on data whose labels are not tied to the source,"* they may ask why the 10-route low-label training comparison was not also run in-domain on an unconfounded dataset (e.g., training low-label routes directly on *iSarcasmEval*'s training split rather than using it solely for zero-shot transfer). Explicitly stating in Section I, Section V (Limitations), and Section VI that *News Headlines* was chosen as the primary training testbed specifically because recent semi-supervised sarcasm architectures [1, 3] claimed their gains on it—while acknowledging as a limitation in Section V that the 10-route comparison was executed only on an outlet-confounded corpus—ensures reviewers view the single-testbed design as a necessary, controlled audit of published claims.
     2. *Why do generative feature-space routes (GAN-BERT, diffusion fakes) add nothing while MLM succeeds?* For non-NLP reviewers familiar with GANs and diffusion models in computer vision or signal processing, it may not be immediately obvious in Sections I–II that GAN-BERT [2] and the diffusion generator operate only on pooled 768-dimensional `[CLS]` sentence vectors, whereas MLM adapts token-level representations across all transformer layers. Highlighting this architectural distinction briefly in Section I or Section VI makes the empirical outcome much more intuitive to a general AI audience.
     3. *Clarifying Terminology and Acronyms on First Use:* In the Abstract and Section I, the phrase *"predictions fixed before each experiment"* / *"predictions fixed in advance"* refers to pre-registered directional experimental hypotheses, but to a general ML reader "predictions" typically means model output probabilities ($\hat{y}$). Phrasing this as *"pre-specified experimental hypotheses"* (or *"directional predictions written down before each experiment"*) avoids ambiguity. Additionally, the acronym **FGM** is used in the Abstract (*"adversarial training (FGM)"*) and Section II (*"FGM [15] was reported..."*) without ever spelling out *Fast Gradient Method* in the body text (it appears only in the title of Reference [16]), and *iSarcasmEval* is mentioned at the end of the Abstract without context. Spelling out *Fast Gradient Method (FGM)* on first mention and briefly identifying *iSarcasmEval* in the Abstract as an author-labeled Twitter sarcasm benchmark will improve accessibility for non-NLP reviewers.

### [Experimental Design, Route Re-implementations, Fairness of Comparison & Statistical Protocol] (Pages: 2)
#### Potential Mistakes and Improvements

1. **Asymmetry in Unlabeled Data Coverage and Step Budgets Between Single-Stage Semi-Supervised Routes and Two-Stage Pretraining (Sections III-B, III-C, and IV-A):**
   * **Potential Concern:** Section III-C specifies that all fine-tuning runs use batch size 32 and a fixed step budget of $\max(200, 10 \text{ passes over } L)$. This unified schedule interacts asymmetrically with single-stage semi-supervised routes that consume the unlabeled pool $U$ during task training versus two-stage pretraining routes that consume $U$ beforehand:
     * *Single-stage joint semi-supervised routes (GAN-BERT A4/A5, Diffusion fakes A24, and Diffusion consistency A25):* At the 1% label budget ($|L| = 228$, $|U| = 22{,}572$), 10 passes over $L$ corresponds to $\lceil 2{,}280 / 32 \rceil = 72$ batches, so the $\max(200, \dots)$ floor allocates exactly 200 optimizer steps. Assuming one unlabeled batch of size 32 is drawn alongside one labeled batch per step, 200 steps exposes these models to at most $200 \times 32 = 6{,}400$ unlabeled headlines—only **28.4% of a single pass (epoch)** over $U$ (which requires $\lceil 22{,}572 / 32 \rceil = 706$ batches per pass). Even under the development check mentioned in Section IV-A ("three times more training steps," i.e., 600 steps), the model completes only $\sim 85\%$ of a single pass over $U$. At the 10% label budget ($|L| = 2{,}280$, $|U| = 20{,}520$), 10 passes over $L$ is $\approx 713$ steps, or roughly $1.1$ passes over $U$. In the original GAN-BERT formulation [2], labeled instances are replicated within each epoch so that one training epoch equals a full pass over $U$, and training runs for multiple full epochs over $U$ so the generator–discriminator feature-matching game can stabilize and traverse the unlabeled distribution; restricting the generator–discriminator game to 200–600 total updates may leave the generator under-converged and contribute to the $-2.3$-point degradation and high variance ($\pm 2.7$) in `A5` at 1%.
     * *Two-stage pretraining routes (MLM A20 and JEPA A22) and Self-Training (A6):* By contrast, MLM and JEPA run for 10,000 pretraining steps at batch size 64 ($640{,}000$ headline exposures, or **$\approx 28.1$ full passes** over the 22,800 training headlines) *prior* to the 200 fine-tuning steps—receiving 50 times more gradient updates on the unlabeled text. Similarly, Self-Training (A6) evaluates the A1 teacher across the entirety of $U$ and then trains a fresh student model on $L$ plus high-confidence pseudo-labeled examples from $U$ (where it is also ambiguous whether the student's step budget remains capped at 200 steps at 1% or scales to 10 passes over the expanded labeled + pseudo-labeled set).
   * **Impact & Suggested Improvement:** Anchoring the fine-tuning step budget strictly to passes over $L$ creates a substantial compute and data-exposure disparity that may starve single-stage joint semi-supervised baselines of the unlabeled pool. Clarify in Sections III-B and III-C how unlabeled batches from $U$ are paired with $L$ per step in A4, A5, A24, and A25, confirm whether the generator and discriminator losses stabilized during the $3\times$ step check, and state how the step budget $\max(200, 10 \text{ passes over } L)$ applies to the student training stage of A6 and the synthetic batch in A10. Furthermore, either verify whether GAN-BERT and consistency training improve when allowed multiple full passes over $U$ (matching the epoch structure of [2]), or explicitly acknowledge this 0.28-pass vs. 28-pass exposure gap when interpreting why single-stage adversarial and consistency routes fail to extract signal from $U$.

2. **Restriction of Helper Heads to Labeled Data $L$ in Arm A2 vs. Full Semi-Supervised Multi-Task Learning in Arm A5 and [3] (Section III-B and Table I):**
   * **Potential Concern:** Section III-B defines the standalone helper-head route as *"Helper heads (A2): sentiment and emotion heads trained on $L$ with teacher labels, loss weight 0.3,"* and defines the combined route as *"A5 adds the helper heads below (our re-implementation of the combined model [3])."* In Table I, A2 is listed as `Helper heads on L (H)` without the `(U)` marker, whereas A5 (`GAN-BERT + helper heads (U)`) carries `(U)`. Because the sentiment and emotion targets are pseudo-labels generated by off-the-shelf pretrained teacher classifiers [13], [14] rather than human annotations, they are available for all 22,572 unlabeled headlines in $U$ at zero human-labeling cost:
     * First, it is ambiguous from Section III-B whether Arm A5 computes the auxiliary sentiment and emotion losses across both $L$ and $U$, or restricts the helper-head losses strictly to the 228 examples in $L$ while only the GAN discriminator reads $U$.
     * Second, restricting A2 (and the stacked combinations A30 and A36) to $L$ tests auxiliary sentiment/emotion supervision purely as a low-data multi-task regularizer on the tiny labeled subset, rather than evaluating whether multi-task distillation on teacher-labeled $U$ transfers useful signal from the unlabeled pool.
   * **Impact & Suggested Improvement:** Explicitly state in Section III-B whether A5 applies the teacher sentiment and emotion losses to $U$ as well as $L$, and briefly explain why A2 was restricted to $L$ rather than evaluated across $U$.

3. **Architectural Warm-Start Advantage and Upstream Seed Parity in the MLM (A20) vs. Text-JEPA (A22) Comparison (Sections III-B, IV-A, and IV-B):**
   * **Potential Concern:** Section III-B compares 10,000 steps of MLM (A20) against 10,000 steps of a text adaptation of JEPA (A22) at fixed settings (batch size 64, learning rate $10^{-4}$), leading to the conclusion in Section IV-A that *"Predicting masked words beats predicting hidden representations here"* based on the 1.75-point (1%) and 1.17-point (10%) gaps. Two methodological asymmetries affect this comparison:
     * *Warm-start vs. cold-start head initialization:* When continuing pretraining from `bert-base-uncased`, `bert-base-uncased` was originally pretrained for 1,000,000 steps with the exact 15% Masked Language Modeling objective and already possesses representations aligned with token reconstruction (as well as a converged MLM output head, assuming the pretrained head weights were retained). Conversely, A22 introduces a newly altered masking scheme (two contiguous spans covering 25% of the words), a randomly initialized latent predictor network, and an exponentially moving average (EMA) target encoder task that `bert-base-uncased` was never trained to perform. Training an uninitialized predictor head from scratch alongside the backbone at the same $10^{-4}$ learning rate requires spending part of the 10,000-step budget warming up the predictor and adapting to a new objective regime.
     * *Upstream pretraining seed parity:* In Section IV-B, upstream pretraining stability is verified for MLM across two additional pretraining seeds (three upstream runs total, whose 5-seed mean F1 scores at 1% span 0.83 points: `+7.42`, `+7.72`, and `+6.89` over BERT). However, as noted in Section V, JEPA (`A22`) was only pretrained with a single upstream seed. Comparing a single JEPA pretraining run against MLM across five downstream fine-tuning seeds captures only downstream fine-tuning variance conditional on one fixed upstream checkpoint.
   * **Impact & Suggested Improvement:** Comparing 10,000 steps of native, warm-started MLM against 10,000 steps of cold-started predictor training on a single upstream seed conflates the intrinsic utility of the pretraining objective with cold-start adaptation overhead and upstream seed variance. Specify in Section III-B whether A20 retains BERT's pretrained MLM decoder head, and either run two additional pretraining seeds for JEPA (~35–44 minutes per run on a T4 GPU) or explicitly qualify in Section IV-A that the MLM-vs.-JEPA comparison reflects downstream fine-tuning variance conditional on a single pretraining seed and benefits from reusing BERT's native prediction head.

4. **Statistical Protocol: Test-Set Bootstrap Resampling vs. Training-Split Variance and Undefined Contrast Families for Holm's Correction (Sections III-C, IV-A, and IV-C):**
   * **Potential Concern:** Two methodological details in the statistical testing protocol in Section III-C warrant clarification:
     1. *Conditioning on five fixed training splits vs. training-subset variance:* Seeds 0–4 vary the randomly drawn labeled subset $L$ (only 228 headlines at 1%), model initialization, and batch order. Significance testing between two routes uses a *"paired bootstrap over test headlines [24] (10,000 resamples, the same resample for all seeds, difference of seed-averaged scores, 95% interval)."* Resampling solely over the 2,851 test headlines while taking the fixed average across the 5 seeds measures test-set sampling uncertainty ($\text{Var}_{\text{test}}$) conditioned on those 5 specific training splits, treating the 5 random draws of $L$ as fixed. At the 1% budget, seed-to-seed variance across different random 228-headline draws of $L$ ($\text{Var}_{\text{seed}}$) is substantial (in Table I, across-seed standard deviations reach $\pm 1.2\%$ for A1, $\pm 2.7\%$ for A5, and $\pm 4.8\%$ for A25), meaning that test-only bootstrap confidence intervals ($\approx \pm 0.8$ points) and small $p$-values (such as $p < 10^{-4}$ for A5 vs. A1 in Section IV-A) can be much tighter than the variability across random training splits.
     2. *Undefined composition of contrast families for Holm's correction and completeness of Table II:* Section III-C states that Holm's step-down correction [25] is applied *"inside each pre-planned family of contrasts,"* and later sections rely on $p_{\text{Holm}}$ values (e.g., $p_{\text{Holm}} = 0.09$ and $0.20$ in Section IV-C, and $p = 0.034$ in Section IV-D) to judge significance. Because Holm's rejection threshold $\alpha / (m - k + 1)$ depends directly on the family size $m$, the adjusted $p$-values cannot be verified without knowing the exact membership and number of comparisons $m$ inside each pre-planned family (e.g., whether Q1 tests 9 routes against A1 per budget for $m=9$ or across budgets for $m=18$, and whether Table II tests the $m=3$ single-route additions, all $m=7$ non-null combinations against `none`, the interaction contrasts separately, or both budgets jointly for $m=14$). Similarly, $p_{\text{Holm}}$ values are not stated alongside the unadjusted 95% bootstrap confidence intervals for the two surviving combinations at 1% (`H + S` at `+1.05 [0.55, 1.56]` and `H + F + S` at `+0.97 [0.39, 1.54]`), and unlike Table I, Table II omits the 5-seed standard deviations ($\pm\text{s.d.}$) and does not explicitly state which of the three MLM pretraining checkpoints from Section IV-B serves as the base encoder (the `none` score of `87.8` indicates it is the primary `A20` checkpoint).
   * **Impact & Suggested Improvement:** Explicitly state the size ($m$) and membership of each pre-planned contrast family in Section III-C (or in the captions of Tables I and II), report $p_{\text{Holm}}$ alongside the intervals for the surviving combinations in Section IV-C, include $\pm\text{s.d.}$ values in Table II to match Table I, note in the Table II caption that all factorial combinations fine-tune the primary `A20` MLM checkpoint, and clarify that the paired bootstrap intervals quantify test-set sampling uncertainty conditioned on the five nested training splits.

5. **Missing Reproducibility Specifications for Adapted Generative and Self-Supervised Routes (Sections III-B and IV-D):**
   * **Potential Concern:** Even within a concise 4–6 page conference format, several experimental arms introduced in Section III-B omit critical parameters needed for independent reproduction and for evaluating why specific methods underperformed:
